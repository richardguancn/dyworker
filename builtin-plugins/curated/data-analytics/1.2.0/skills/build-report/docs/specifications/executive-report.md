# Decision-focused reports

Use this reference when readers need to decide, prioritize, or communicate a result. It is not a required outline.

Apply the [title and opening principles](../../SKILL.md#report-principles) to a decision-focused report, including the compact date/known-author byline and visible Executive Summary. Put workspace/sample identity and detailed methods below the main findings. Do not make the opening an evidence-free verdict or a methodology recap. A brief one-answer report may use a concise opening instead. This is a reading aid, not a required outline. Use plain language and give each important finding enough context to understand its magnitude, comparison, and consequence. Keep dense methodology, source, freshness, provenance and reproducibility details out of the opening unless the method itself is the question or a methodological caveat changes the answer.

Possible shapes include an answer with one decisive chart, a KPI operating review, a diagnostic memo, or a recommendation with alternatives and tradeoffs. Use metric cards for distinct headline measures when useful, and tables when readers need exact lookup. Neither is mandatory. A recommendation should name a feasible next action and follow from the evidence; monitoring or an unresolved question may be the right ending.

Check that a busy reader can understand the result by scanning the title, opening, and section headings, and can inspect the evidence without relying on hidden process notes. Avoid duplicate summaries, generic advice, and claims stronger than the analysis.

## Synthetic worked example

This festival packet is a worked example, not reusable evidence for other reports. Mixed booking contributed $3,352 after staffing; advance-only sessions contributed $4,315, an observed $963 difference. These are contributions before other costs, not profit. The opening connects findings, evidence and implications while preserving the trial's causal limitation:

### Film festival booking policy for next season

*Should the film festival switch to advance bookings only next season?*

#### Executive Summary

- **Keep walk up tickets next season.** The advance only sessions earned $963 more after staffing, but $675 of that gap came from higher premiere ticket prices. The trial compared different events, so it cannot tell us how much the booking rule itself mattered.
- **The extra contribution came alongside lower attendance.** Advance only sessions filled 306 of 400 seats, compared with 338 of 400 under mixed booking, an 8.0 percentage point occupancy gap. Walk ups accounted for 121 of the mixed sessions' 338 attendees, so removing that route risks excluding spontaneous visitors; the packet does not show how many would book instead.
- **Retain advance booking and test a walk up allocation under comparable conditions.** Staff hours were lower during the trial, and selected comments describe easier preparation and reassurance from reserved seats. Use reservations for planning while measuring whether removing walk ups improves contribution without harming attendance or access.

The $675 comparison holds sold-ticket and refunded-ticket counts and staffing fixed, repricing both sales and refunds at the regular $12 price. It is a price sensitivity calculation; it does not estimate how prices or the booking rule changed demand. In a full report, cite the supplied evidence beside these findings and expand the calculations in the body and Sources.
